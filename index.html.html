YOUR TRAINING GUIDE

Fitness tips ✦

Small habits that build strength, endurance, and consistency.

✳
↗

Progress gradually

Add weight or reps only after you can complete every set with controlled technique. Small, consistent increases beat rushed jumps.

◷

Prioritize recovery

Aim for 7–9 hours of sleep, allow hard-worked muscles time to recover, and adjust training when soreness affects your movement.

◎

Fuel your training

Include protein in meals, eat enough to support your goal, and use your food diary to notice patterns—not to chase perfect days.

♥

Build your engine

Mix easier conversational-pace cardio with harder conditioning. Start conservatively and increase intensity as fitness improves.

◈

Warm up with purpose

Begin with 5–10 minutes of easy movement, then lighter sets of your main lift before working up to heavier weights.

✓

Track consistency

Log workouts and celebrate regular training, improved technique, and energy—not just personal records or scale changes.

Listen to your body: stop exercising if you experience dizziness, chest pain, or unusual symptoms. Seek medical advice for persistent or severe symptoms.

Built for muscle, strength retention, stronger legs and shoulders, and CrossFit-style conditioning.

How to progress

Weeks 1–3 · Establish baseline

Work at ~2–3 reps in reserve (RIR). Start with 2 sets per accessory if recovery is difficult. Improve form and repeatable pacing.

Week 4 · Deload

Reduce lifting sets by roughly 40–50% and conditioning volume by 30–40%; keep movement quality high.

Weeks 5–7 · Build

Progress compound lifts by 2.5–5 lb upper body or 5–10 lb lower body when all prescribed reps are completed with good form. Aim for 1–2 RIR.

Week 8 · Deload

Reduce lifting sets by 40–50% and keep conditioning easy.

Weeks 9–11 · Consolidate

Maintain heavy compound work, build accessory volume as tolerated, and try to improve your conditioning benchmark without sacrificing technique.

Week 12 · Assess

Reduce volume and compare rep PRs, waist, weight trend, and repeatable WOD performance. Avoid testing true maxes when fatigued.

Training guidelines

Warm-up: 5–10 minutes easy movement, mobility for the lifts, then 2–4 lighter ramp-up sets before your first heavy lift.

Rest: 2–3 minutes for compound lifts; 60–90 seconds for accessory work. Leave 1–3 reps in reserve on most sets.

Recovery: Sunday off; sleep 7–9 hours. If you're new to conditioning, make Wednesday an easy zone-2 day and skip Saturday's intensity for the first two weeks.

Progression: When you reach the top of a rep range across all sets with good technique, increase the load next time. Log your weights in the Workouts tab.

This is a general training template, not a medical assessment. Scale exercises to your experience and stop for unusual pain, dizziness, or concerning symptoms.
